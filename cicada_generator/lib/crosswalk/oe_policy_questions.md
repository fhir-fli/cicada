## Long-term aspirin therapy from a problem-list code

CDC's immunization decision-support logic (CDSi) has a patient condition "Receiving long-term aspirin therapy" (CDSi observation 034), used because aspirin with varicella or influenza risks Reye syndrome. ICD-10-CM Z79.82 is "Long term (current) use of aspirin". Is a problem-list entry of Z79.82 sufficient to treat a child or adolescent as receiving long-term aspirin therapy for live influenza vaccine and varicella vaccine decisions, or does the ACIP precaution need more (current active prescription, dose, age)? Answer with the reason and a source.

## Chronic lung disease: does it include upper-airway disease?

CDC's CDSi condition "Chronic lung disease" (CDSi observation 017) is coded by CDC with SNOMED CT concepts that include "Chronic disease of respiratory system" (17097001). That concept also covers chronic sinusitis, chronic rhinitis, chronic tonsillitis and chronic laryngitis. For the ACIP pneumococcal and other recommendations that use "chronic lung disease", should chronic upper-airway conditions such as chronic sinusitis (J32.x), chronic rhinitis (J31.0) or chronic tonsillitis (J35.0x) count? And does asthma count as chronic lung disease there, given CDSi also has a separate condition "Asthma"? Answer with the reason and a source.

## Chronic cardiovascular disease: does it include venous and minor vascular disease?

CDC's CDSi condition "Chronic cardiovascular disease" (CDSi observation 254), used for RSV vaccination in adults, is coded by CDC with SNOMED CT concepts including "Chronic disease of cardiovascular system" (128292002), peripheral vascular disease, pulmonary embolism and portal vein thrombosis. For the ACIP RSV recommendation, should these count: deep vein thrombosis (I80.2, I82.4x), varicose veins of the legs with or without ulcer (I83.x), chronic venous insufficiency (I87.2), hemorrhoids (K64.x), isolated essential hypertension (I10), and cerebrovascular disease such as prior stroke (I63.x, I69.x)? Answer each with the reason and a source.
