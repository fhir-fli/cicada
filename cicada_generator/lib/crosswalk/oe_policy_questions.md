## Transplant ICD-10-CM codes and the three CDSi transplant conditions

CDC's immunization decision-support logic (CDSi) has three transplant conditions:

- "Recipient of a hematopoietic stem cell transplant" (CDSi observation 004): used for Hib, RSV and COVID-19 (revaccination 6 to 12 months after a successful transplant).
- "Solid organ transplantation" (CDSi observation 157): used for 12 vaccines, including as a contraindication to live vaccines (measles, mumps, rubella, varicella, zoster, yellow fever, dengue, typhoid, influenza).
- "Transplantation" (CDSi observation 269): used only for HPV ("Administer to persons who received a transplant"; the 3-dose series for immunocompromised persons).

A clinic system has only the ICD-10-CM code on the problem list. For each code below, which of the three conditions does a patient carrying that code meet, for the purpose of the ACIP recommendations that use them? Answer for every code with the conditions (or none), the reason and a source. The tentative answer is given after each code.

## DiGeorge syndrome

CDSi has two conditions that change live-vaccine and other recommendations: "T-lymphocyte [cell-mediated and humoral] - Complete defects" (CDSi observation 147; examples given: SCID, complete DiGeorge syndrome) and "T-lymphocyte [cell-mediated and humoral] - Partial defects" (CDSi observation 148; examples given: most patients with DiGeorge syndrome, Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome, ataxia-telangiectasia). ICD-10-CM D82.1 "Di George's syndrome" does not say whether the defect is complete or partial. Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome (D82.0) is already counted as a partial defect.

Does a patient whose only record is D82.1 meet the partial-defect condition, the complete-defect condition, or neither, for the ACIP recommendations that use them? Tentative: partial defects, because CDC's own wording says most patients with DiGeorge syndrome have partial defects, and a complete defect needs evidence the code does not carry. Give the reason and a source.
